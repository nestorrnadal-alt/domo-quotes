Completion & Walkthrough

The work is substantially complete when the scope has been performed and the affected areas can be used for their intended purpose. Minor items may remain — meaning items that do not prevent that use and can be corrected without significant disruption.

Domo notifies the Client and the parties walk the work together on a date agreed between them, within five (5) working days of the notice. At the walkthrough they record one written punch list of anything that does not meet the scope. The final payment is due at the walkthrough, except that the Client may hold back the reasonable cost of the punch items — priced by agreement, or by a third-party estimate if the parties cannot agree — and releases that holdback within three (3) working days of those items being corrected. Domo will correct punch items within ten (10) working days unless a part or trade requires longer.

As a backstop only: if fifteen (15) working days pass from Domo's notice without a walkthrough taking place for reasons within the Client's control, and the Client has not given Domo a written list of objections in that time, the work is treated as substantially complete and the final payment becomes due — but the Client's punch-list holdback right and the warranty both survive. Using the finished rooms is not, by itself, acceptance of the work.

Changes to the Work

Any change to the scope, materials, price or schedule must be approved in writing before the additional work begins. Email or WhatsApp is fine — a written approval sent from the Client's email address or phone number listed in the Parties section counts as a signature.

Unless a fixed price is agreed for the change, additional work is billed at $65 per worker-hour including IVU, plus materials at cost plus 15%. Approved changes adjust the total price and the dates above. Domo is not obligated to perform work that has not been approved in writing.

Concealed & Structural Conditions

The scope and price are based on what was visible at the time of quoting. Except where the scope or quote says otherwise, all walls, ceilings and openings in the scope are assumed to be non-structural and non-load-bearing, based on visual inspection only.

If Domo encounters a concealed condition — hidden piping or wiring, water damage, termite or structural damage, unpermitted prior work, or suspected hazardous material — or if any element proves to be load-bearing, Domo stops work in that area, notifies the Client within twenty-four (24) hours with photographs, and prices the additional work as a change order. Structural repair, engineering and hazardous-material abatement are outside this agreement unless the scope or quote includes them, and are the Client's to authorize and pay for. If the Client does not decide within five (5) working days of the notice, the schedule extends by the delay and the remobilization fee under Schedule may apply.

Permits & Licenses

Permits and government fees are the Client's responsibility and are not included in the price. At the Client's written request, Domo will obtain permits as the Client's agent, and the Client reimburses the actual fees plus a handling charge of $150 per permit. The Client confirms it owns the property or has authority to permit the work, and will obtain any homeowner-association, condominium or landlord approvals required. Neither party is responsible for delays caused by permitting authorities or associations.

Materials, Title & Risk of Loss

Principal materials — with brand, model, color and specification where applicable — are listed in the quote delivered with this agreement. Domo furnishes materials of good quality suitable for the work unless the Client specifies otherwise in writing. Where a specified item is unavailable or discontinued, Domo notifies the Client and may substitute an item of equal or better quality and specification.

Title and risk of loss for materials pass to the Client on delivery to the site or on payment, whichever is earlier — except that Domo remains responsible for loss or damage caused by Domo, its employees or its subcontractors. Domo will store materials reasonably at the site. The Client's property insurance covers materials on site against theft, fire and weather.

A material price increase adjusts the price only where it exceeds 10% of that item's quoted cost, arises after a Client-caused delay of more than ten (10) working days in signing, selection or ordering, and is shown to the Client with supplier documentation before the material is ordered.

Site Access & Client Responsibilities

Domo works from 7:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m., Monday through Friday. Saturday and evening work is by agreement.

Before the start date the Client clears the work area of furniture, belongings and anything fragile or valuable, and discloses in writing any condition it knows of behind the walls or under the floors. Domo is not responsible for pre-existing conditions, for damage to items the Client failed to remove after being asked, or for ordinary dust and vibration inherent in demolition work. This does not excuse damage caused by Domo's own negligence.

Where the Client supplies appliances, fixtures or finishes, they must be on site and complete before the week in which they are needed. Material and color selections are due at least five (5) working days before they are needed.

Subcontractors & Insurance

Domo may perform the work through its own personnel or through qualified subcontractors, and remains responsible to the Client for all of the work either way.

Domo carries commercial general liability insurance, maintains workers' compensation coverage (CFSE) as required by Puerto Rico law, and requires each subcontractor on this project to carry its own coverage or to be covered under Domo's. Certificates are available on request. Domo indemnifies the Client against any claim by a Domo worker or subcontractor for injury on this project. The Client maintains its own property and homeowner's insurance in force throughout the work.

Cleanup

Domo removes its debris and leaves the work area in broom-clean condition — swept, with construction debris, packaging and offcuts removed from the property — at completion. Disposal of construction debris is included where listed in the scope; otherwise it is billed at cost. Final detailed cleaning and painting-grade dust removal are not included.

Workmanship Warranty

Domo stands behind its work. For one (1) year from substantial completion, Domo will correct at no charge any part of the completed work that fails because of defective workmanship — electrical connections, plumbing connections, block and structural-adjacent work, and anything that stops functioning as intended.

Cosmetic and finish items are covered for ninety (90) days: plaster and joint cracking, nail pops, minor settlement movement, and surface finish. These are the things a building does on its own as it settles, and ninety days is enough to show a real defect.

Materials, appliances and fixtures carry the manufacturer's warranty, which Domo passes through to the Client.

Neither warranty covers damage from misuse, neglect, normal wear, moisture or weather, alterations or repairs by others, Client-supplied materials, or work Domo was directed to perform over its own written recommendation. Cracking, nail pops and movement that appear after the ninety-day cosmetic period are treated as normal settlement and are not covered. Notify Domo in writing within the applicable period and allow reasonable access to inspect and correct.

This warranty is in addition to, and does not replace or limit, any right the Client has under Puerto Rico law, including the warranty against hidden defects and the builder's liability for a structure under the Civil Code.

Responsibility & Limitation of Liability

Each party is responsible for its own negligence and will indemnify the other against third-party claims arising from its own negligent acts or omissions in connection with the work.

Please read this section. It limits what you can recover from Domo.

The limit. Except for the four things listed next, the most Domo can be required to pay under this agreement is the contract price.

What the limit does not apply to. Claims covered by Domo's general liability insurance, which responds up to its own policy limits; injury to a person; damage caused by Domo's gross negligence or willful misconduct; and Domo's indemnity obligations under the first paragraph of this section.

Knock-on losses. Neither party pays the other for losses that follow from a problem rather than being the problem itself — lost rental or business income, loss of use of the property, or a drop in property value.

Puerto Rico law comes first. Nothing in this agreement limits a right or a liability that Puerto Rico law does not allow to be limited or waived.

Cancellation & Termination

Your right to cancel. If the Client signed this agreement somewhere other than Domo's place of business, the Client may cancel it without cost, penalty or obligation within three (3) business days of signing, by sending written notice to Domo at the mailing address or email address in the Parties section, or by using the Notice of Cancellation form delivered with this agreement. On a timely cancellation Domo refunds the full deposit within ten (10) days and Domo bears the cost of anything it chose to order during those three days.

Cancellation after the three-day period, before work begins. The Client may still cancel. Domo retains only its documented costs actually incurred — scheduling, evaluation, and non-returnable materials already ordered — and refunds the rest of the deposit within ten (10) days, with receipts.

Ending the agreement once work has begun. The Client may end this agreement at any time on written notice, and pays for the work performed and materials supplied through that date, plus any non-cancellable commitments, plus Domo's documented direct costs of demobilizing. Domo may end this agreement on written notice if the property is unsafe or if a condition makes the work impracticable, and refunds anything paid for work not performed.

Default. Either party may terminate for material breach if the breach is not cured within ten (10) calendar days of written notice. Non-payment is a material breach. If Domo is the party in default, the Client may terminate, recover any amount paid for work not performed, and recover the reasonable additional cost of having another contractor complete the scope, up to the unpaid balance of the contract price.

Liens

Domo and its suppliers retain any lien rights provided by Puerto Rico law until paid in full. On final payment, Domo provides a written release of lien rights for the work covered by that payment.

Notices

Notices of breach, suspension or termination must be in writing and sent by email — to Domo at info@domoyourhome.com, and to the Client at the email address in the Parties section. An emailed notice is effective when sent. Each party keeps its email address current and tells the other in writing if it changes. A notice to cancel under the three-day right described above may also be sent by mail to Domo's address in the Parties section. Day-to-day communication and change-order approvals may be by email or WhatsApp.

General

Domo performs as an independent contractor, not an employee or partner of the Client. This agreement, together with the quote delivered with it and any approved change orders, is the entire agreement between the parties and supersedes prior discussions, proposals and estimates. Neither party may assign this agreement without the other's written consent, which will not be unreasonably withheld. If any provision is unenforceable, the remainder stays in effect and the unenforceable provision is applied to the fullest extent the law allows. This agreement may be signed electronically, and an electronic signature has the same effect as a handwritten one.

This agreement is governed by the laws of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico. The parties will first try to resolve any dispute in good faith directly, within fifteen (15) days of written notice of the dispute. Unresolved disputes go to the Court of First Instance, San Juan Judicial Region. The prevailing party in an action to enforce this agreement is entitled to its reasonable attorney's fees and costs. Nothing here prevents either party from bringing a claim before the Puerto Rico Department of Consumer Affairs (DACo).
